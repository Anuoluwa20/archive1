**The headline finding.** All 22 entries in the two primary categories (A,
typographic/wordmark; B, archival and editorial systems) are **type-led, and not
one of them depends on a symbol.** The two most instructive systems in the whole
corpus — Tate (Wolff Olins, 2000) and Uli practice in Igbo art — both derive their
power from *one fixed invariant that varies its state or ordering*, not from a
mark. For ARCHIVE the identity should be **typographic, singular and stateful**;
the symbol, monogram and geometric routes are all second-order here and should be
treated as optional extensions, never as the primary asset.

**Quantitative context.** Brand New's own tag counts (observed 15 Oct 2026) show
where identity practice actually sits in this sector:

- `monogram` — 880 entries · `wordmark` — 433 · `geometric` — 312
- `museum` — 205 · `pattern` — 147 · `sans serif` — 2,425 · `serif` — 711
- `africa` — 20 · `heritage` — 2 · `editorial` — 2 · `minimal` — 17

Read that as: **the "heritage" register is what people reach for when they have
nothing specific** (2 tagged entries in a 68-entry corpus of thousands), while
`africa` is rare enough (20) that a genuinely African-specific identity is a
differentiation opportunity, not a crowded field.

## 3. Category A — Typographic / wordmark (institutional)

The core category for this brand. Every entry here is a museum, library or
national collection whose entire identity is carried by letterforms.

- [Museum of West African Art (MOWAA)](https://wearemowaa.org/) — the single most relevant reference in this document: a real West African museum (Benin City, Nigeria; established 2020) whose identity is a designed wordmark system rather than a heritage emblem. Why it works: it treats a West African institution as a contemporary design problem, with no exoticising device anywhere in the identity. — fit: **strong** — evidence: site title "MOWAA - Museum of West African Art" (observed on site); "New Logo and Identity for Museum of West African Art by Human After All", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_museum_of_west_african_art_by_human_after_all.php (tagline as published: "Ahead of the Curves"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_West_African_Art (Established 2020; Location Benin City, Nigeria) — shot: n/a
- [MoMA (The Museum of Modern Art)](https://www.moma.org/) — why it works: a single logotype held unchanged for six decades proves that a wordmark can *be* the institution's equity, with no symbol required. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "The Museum of Modern Art in New York City hired Ivan Chermayeff (NY-based graphic designer) in 1964 to design the museum's logotype. The recognizable result, MoMA, used Franklin Gothic No. 2 and has endured for over 60 years." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Gothic — shot: n/a
- [Whitney Museum of American Art](https://whitney.org/) — why it works: a typographic identity that behaves responsively — the mark changes form as context changes while remaining unmistakably itself, which is exactly the mechanic a "past → present" narrative needs. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "The group's most notable projects include the 2013 logotype and brand identity for the Whitney Museum in New York", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Jetset (which cites Dezeen, 21 May 2013, and It's Nice That, 23 May 2013) — shot: n/a
- [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/) — **the key structural precedent.** Why it works: one wordmark issued as a *family of registered states* — standard, blurred, faded and halftone — so the identity itself argues that a thing can be several states at once and still be one thing. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "The Tate logo was designed by international brand consultancy Wolff Olins in 2000 as part of a larger rebranding effort focused around the idea 'look again, think again.' The museum uses a range of logos that move in and out of focus… Variations include a standard logo, a blurred version, a faded version and a halftone version consisting of dots rather than smooth fading. An update on the brand, designed by North, was released in 2016." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate — shot: n/a
- [V&A (Victoria and Albert Museum)](https://www.vam.ac.uk/) — why it works: a name-as-mark where two letters and an ampersand carry a whole family of museums; the economy is the point. — fit: **partial** (the "V&A" contraction doesn't transfer — "ARCHIVE" has no natural contraction, so the lesson is economy, not form) — evidence: Wikipedia captions its infobox logo "Logo introduced in 1989", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_and_Albert_Museum — shot: n/a
- [Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/) — why it works: the identity was *reissued with the institution* — new name, new wordmark and new site launched together in June 2014 — rather than being retrofitted to old positioning. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "In June 2014, the museum's name was changed again to Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum. A new graphic identity, wordmark, and new website were launched on this day. The identity was designed by Eddie Opara of Pentagram." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Hewitt,_Smithsonian_Design_Museum — shot: n/a
  - *Unverified lead, flagged:* it is widely reported that Cooper Hewitt's bespoke typeface was drawn from forms in the museum's own collection. I could not confirm this from a retrievable source in this environment. If true it is a direct model for ARCHIVE (§14, R2). **Do not state it on the page until sourced.**
- [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/) — why it works: the largest archival institution in the anglophone world carries its identity on a wordmark alone; typography is the whole brand. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for Library of Congress by Pentagram", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_library_of_congress_by_pentagram.php — shot: n/a
- National Gallery of Art (Washington, DC) — why it works: the same studio (Pentagram) solving a near-identical problem — a national collection with no reliable symbol — through typography and a system, not a device. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for National Gallery of Art by Pentagram", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_national_gallery_of_art_by_pentagram.php (homepage `nga.gov` returned 403 from this environment; not cited) — shot: n/a
- Art Gallery of New South Wales — why it works: a location-specific cultural institution (Sydney) whose identity is a type-led frame rather than a generic "art" device. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for Art Gallery of New South Wales by Mucho and In-house", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_art_gallery_of_new_south_wales_by_mucho_and_in_house.php (homepage did not resolve from this environment; not cited) — shot: n/a
- [National Museums Liverpool](https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/) — why it works: a group of museums unified by one wordmark logic — the model to copy if ARCHIVE ever spans four discipline "wings". — fit: **strong** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for National Museums Liverpool by SomeOne", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_national_museums_liverpool_by_someone.php — shot: n/a
- [National Gallery of Ireland](https://www.nationalgallery.ie/) — why it works: a national collection identity that resists the beige/heraldry default and reads as publishing-adjacent. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for National Gallery of Ireland by True North", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_national_gallery_of_ireland_by_true_north.php — shot: n/a
- [National Gallery in Prague](https://www.ngprague.cz/) — why it works: a central-European national gallery identity by a studio with a strong typographic record — evidence that "national collection" and "typographic" are compatible at scale. — fit: **partial** (reference for range, not for form) — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for National Gallery in Prague by Studio Najbrt", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_national_gallery_in_prague_by_studio_najbrt.php — shot: n/a

## 4. Category B — Archival & editorial typographic identity systems

Mastheads, cultural journals, photography archives. This category answers the
SKU's positioning line directly: *"a cultural institution that behaves like a
contemporary publication."*

- [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/) — why it works: a masthead can be an archival object *and* a live identity for a century, because the letters stay and the system around them changes. — fit: **strong** (as principle; the specific face is not transferable) — evidence: "The New Yorker's signature display typeface, used for its nameplate and headlines… is named Irvin, named after its creator, the designer-illustrator Rea Irvin. The body text of all articles is set in Adobe Caslon." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker — shot: n/a
- [The Gentlewoman](https://thegentlewoman.co.uk/) — why it works: a masthead-only identity with no symbol whatsoever, which nonetheless reads as an institution rather than a startup — precisely the register SKU §6 asks for. — fit: **strong** — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [Fantastic Man](https://www.fantasticman.com/) — why it works: the same type-only discipline applied to editorial and archive content; the identity is a typographic *decision*, repeated, not a drawn thing. — fit: **strong** — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [Apartamento](https://apartamentomagazine.com/) — why it works: an editorial brand that functions as an archive of lived material culture; its identity survives on wordmark + a strict grid, with no decorative device. — fit: **strong** — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [Aperture](https://aperture.org/) — why it works: a photography institution that is simultaneously a magazine, a book publisher and an archive, and carries all three on one wordmark plus issue/volume numbering. — fit: **strong** (the closest existing analogue to ARCHIVE's scope) — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [Magnum Photos](https://www.magnumphotos.com/) — why it works: a photographer-owned archive whose identity is a wordmark and a credit line — the credit line is doing the branding. Relevant to SKU §8 ("real credits on the page"). — fit: **strong** — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [The Republic](https://republic.com.ng/) — why it works: a contemporary Nigerian long-form journal; its wordmark is shipped as a single SVG asset and reused in the nav and mobile nav, i.e. the identity is a typographic file, not a drawn logo. This is the local, current register ARCHIVE should sit alongside. — fit: **strong** — evidence: `<img alt="The Republic" src="/images/shared/menu/menu-logo.svg">` (observed in site source); a second mark `alt="RPUBLC Inc Logo"` shows an archive/library sub-brand pattern in use — shot: n/a
- [Nka: Journal of Contemporary African Art](https://www.dukeupress.edu/nka) — why it works: the scholarly journal of contemporary African art — curatorial, cited, unexotic — proof that an African art institution's identity can read as academic publishing rather than tourism. — fit: **strong** — evidence: "Nka" journal page, Duke University Press (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [n+1](https://www.nplusonemag.com/) — why it works: an editorial identity built from punctuation and hierarchy; the "n+1" mark shows a two-character typographic asset carrying a whole publication. — fit: **partial** (register is right; the topical world is not) — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- [The National Archives (UK)](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/) — why it works: a state archive that presents itself as a service and a catalogue, not a temple; institutional restraint is the identity. — fit: **partial** (right instinct, but the UK civil-service register is too cold for a brand that must feel "warm, not dusty", SKU §6) — evidence: live site (observed on site) — shot: n/a

## 5. Category C — Symbol / mark

Deliberately the weakest category for this brand. Included because the brief
requires coverage, and because the *reason* these marks work is instructive: in
almost every strong case the mark is an abstraction of the institution's own
letterforms or its own building — not an imported metaphor.

- [Serpentine Galleries](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/) — why it works: a gallery with a recognised name and no pictorial device; the identity is credited on-site as a studio commission, which is the Sourcing discipline ARCHIVE should copy for its own brand credits. — fit: **strong** (as a precedent for *no symbol*) — evidence: site footer reads "Visual identity by Hingston Studio. Site designed by The Unloved. Developed by Effect." (observed on site) — shot: n/a
- New Museum — why it works: a museum whose whole proposition is "new" avoids a pictorial mark, which is the only honest answer for an institution defined by negation of the old. — fit: **partial** — evidence: "New Identity for New Museum by Athletics", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_identity_for_new_museum_by_athletics.php (homepage did not resolve from this environment; not cited) — shot: n/a
- Museum of Ethnography (Budapest) — why it works: it abstracts the museum's own new building into the identity rather than borrowing from the cultures it holds — a crucial distinction for a museum that displays other people's material. — fit: **partial** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for Museum of Ethnography by Explicit Design Studio & de_form studio", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_museum_of_ethnography_by_explicit_design_studio_and_de_form_studio.php (homepage did not resolve from this environment; not cited). **See also §11.7 for why this institution is also a cautionary case.** — shot: n/a
- [Academy Museum of Motion Pictures](https://www.academymuseum.org/) — why it works: a single-subject museum (film) making a mark that is legible as a museum first and a film reference second. — fit: **partial** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for Academy Museum of Motion Pictures by Vedros Studio", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_academy_museum_of_motion_pictures_by_vedros_studio.php — shot: n/a
- [Field Museum](https://www.fieldmuseum.org/) — why it works: a 130-year-old natural-history museum repositioned without inventing a heritage device. — fit: **partial** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for Field Museum by Leo Burnett Department of Design", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_field_museum_by_leo_burnett_department_of_design.php — shot: n/a
- [Royal Ontario Museum](https://www.rom.on.ca/) — why it works: the mark is derived from the museum's own architecture — again, source material is the institution, not a metaphor. — fit: **partial** — evidence: "New Logo and Identity for the Royal Ontario Museum (ROM) by Leo Design", Brand New, https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_or_royal_ontario_museum_rom_by_leo_design.php — shot: n/a

## 9. Category G — Expressive / experimental

Included because SKU §7 requires motion, and because a *stateful* wordmark (§14, R1)
is technically an expressive solution. The risk here is that expressive systems
read as festival or agency work — a specific SKU §6 exclusion — so each entry is
judged on whether its expressiveness is contained.

## 11. Category I — ANTI-PATTERNS

These are the clichés of cultural/heritage branding, catalogued so they can be
excluded by name. Each is cited to a real, authoritative source; each is also
diagrammed abstractly in `artifacts/04-brand/assets/ANTI-PATTERN-SCHEMATICS.svg`.

> Method note: these are **archetypes**, not accusations. Several of the emblems
> below are wholly appropriate for their own purpose — a UN flag needs to be
> secular, universal and instantly reproducible. The anti-pattern is *borrowing
> that register for a curatorial brand*, where it reads as administrative rather
> than editorial.

### 11.1 Globe-in-a-ring (the "universal culture" mark)

- [United Nations emblem](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-emblem-and-flag) — archetype: a world map in azimuthal equidistant projection centred on the North Pole, inscribed in a wreath of crossed olive branches, gold on smoke-blue. Source states the design was created by a team led by Oliver Lincoln Lundquist at the 1945 UNCIO, and that the projection "extends to 60 degrees south latitude, and includes five concentric circles." — fit: **anti-pattern** — evidence: the page above (retrieved 15 Oct 2026) — shot: n/a
- Why it is a trap for ARCHIVE: it says "international, peaceful, generic" — the exact opposite of SKU §6's demand to be "specific to Nigeria, not generically 'African'". It would make ARCHIVE look like an NGO, not a museum.

### 11.2 Continent-silhouette-in-concentric-circles

- [Emblem of the African Union](https://au.int/en) — archetype, per the AU's own descriptive text as reported: "a gold ribbon bearing small interlocking red rings, from which palm leaves shoot up around an outer gold circle and an inner green circle, within which is a gold representation of Africa." — fit: **anti-pattern** — evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union (Symbols section) — shot: n/a
- Why it is a trap: it collapses 54 countries and hundreds of visual cultures into one silhouette. Adopting it would contradict the SKU's "represent breadth across region, era and discipline" risk mitigation (SKU §10) and the "specific to Nigeria" requirement.

### 11.3 Green-white-green used as decoration

- [Coat of arms of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Nigeria) — archetype: a black shield with a wavy white pall (the Niger/Benue confluence at Lokoja), two horses as supporters, an eagle as crest, and — critically — "the green and white twists of the torse on the top of the shield represent the colours of the Nigerian Flag", with the national flower *Costus spectabilis* at the base. — fit: **anti-pattern** — evidence: the article above, which quotes the official blazon — shot: n/a
- Why it is a trap: SKU §6 explicitly bans "green-white-green shorthand used as decoration". A curatorial brand that adopts state heraldry inherits the state's register — formal, bureaucratic, ceremonial — and stops being a publication.

### 11.4 Pattern-block shorthand ("kente-ish geometric blocks")

- [Kente cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente_cloth) — archetype: the reduction of woven-strip logic into a repeatable colour grid. The article documents the real situation: kente production is classified into authentic hand-woven cloth, "kente print produced by brands such as Vlisco and Akosombo Textile Ltd", and "mass-produced kente pattern typically produced in China for West Africans"; and it notes the designs are *named* — from "proverbs, historical events, important chiefs, queen mothers, and plants" — so that "there is often little correlation between appearance and name." — fit: **anti-pattern** — evidence: the article above (Production and Characteristics sections) — shot: n/a
- Why it is a trap, in one line: **the pattern is the part with no information in it.** The names, the makers and the weaving logic are where the meaning lives — and a decorative grid throws all three away. Note also that kente is Ghanaian/Asante, so using it for a Nigerian institution is a second-order error.

### 11.5 Faux-tribal mask device

- No authoritative source will ever document an invented mark, so this one is cited by *contrast*: the real, documented Nigerian symbol system it displaces is [Nsibidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsibidi) — "a system of symbols or proto-writing developed by the Ejagham people in the cross river region of Nigeria and south-western pa[rt]…", with a period of "circa 400 AD – present", and documented child systems **Anaforuana symbols (Cuba)** and **Veve symbols (Haiti)**. — fit: **anti-pattern** (the invented version) / **strong** (the real one, §12) — evidence: the article above — shot: n/a
- Why it is a trap: an invented tribal mark cannot be credited, cannot be explained, and cannot survive a single question from a researcher — one of ARCHIVE's five audience groups (SKU §3).

### 11.6 The standard museum template (beige, serif, grid-of-cards)

- Named explicitly in SKU §6 as a must-avoid, and diagrammed as panel 6 of the schematics sheet. The positive counterexamples are §8 above (National Gallery of Canada, Toledo, Baltimore, Norton). — fit: **anti-pattern** — evidence: SKU §6 (verbatim: *"The standard museum template: beige, serif, grid-of-cards, 'collection' copy."*) — shot: n/a

### 11.7 The ethnography-museum gaze

- Museum of Ethnography, Budapest — the institution type, not the identity, is the caution: a museum that holds other people's material culture must not present it as specimen. The identity solution noted in §5 (abstract the *building*, not the *cultures*) is precisely the correct answer. — fit: **anti-pattern** (the gaze) — evidence: https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_museum_of_ethnography_by_explicit_design_studio_and_de_form_studio.php — shot: n/a
- Why it matters for ARCHIVE: ARCHIVE is Nigerian, presenting Nigerian history to Nigerians and the diaspora — it is not an outside institution looking in. But the risk is real whenever a poster, sleeve or textile is captioned as "African art". SKU §2 and §8 require discipline here.

## 12. Specificity bank — documented Nigerian visual vocabularies

This section exists because the anti-patterns above are all *generic*. The
brief's "must avoid" list is only actionable if a specific alternative exists.
These are the specific, citable alternatives. **All verified from source. Using
any of them on the page requires a visible credit.**

- **Nsibidi** — "a system of symbols or proto-writing developed by the Ejagham people in the cross river region of Nigeria and south-western pa[rt]", period "circa 400 AD – present". Documented descendant systems: **Anaforuana (Cuba)** and **Veve (Haiti)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsibidi
  - *Why this is the strongest single find of the research*: it is a Nigerian sign system with a **documented, verified diaspora continuity** in the Caribbean. That is a real "past → present" story that can be cited rather than asserted — which is exactly the brief's guiding principle.
- **Adire** — Yoruba resist-dyed textile art; "the tradition of indigo dyeing goes back centuries in West Africa. The earliest known example is a cap from the Dogon kingdom in Mali dating to the 11th century, dyed in the oniko style." The article also documents real decline: by the late 1930s "the spread of synthetic indigo, caustic soda, and an influx of new less skilled entrants caused quality problems and a still-present collapse in demand", with complex starch-resist designs produced only "until the early 1970s". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adire_(textile_art)
  - *Why it matters*: adire gives ARCHIVE a **verifiable preservation case study** — a craft with a documented loss event — which is far more moving than a decorative pattern, and matches the "closing / reinforce preservation" section (SKU §5, item 07).
- **Aso oke** — Yoruba hand-woven cloth, "known originally as Òfì"; woven in Yorubaland within "today's Nigeria, Benin and Togo", with the town of **Iseyin** (Oyo State) "historically noted as a major production hub"; traditionally woven by men; historically made from "native cotton, silk from caterpillar cocoons, and threads dyed with indigo"; and — most usefully — described as a form of **"verbal communication with each design telling a story"**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aso_oke
  - *Why it matters*: "each design tells a story" is the design principle ARCHIVE needs. It also names the loom toolset (Akata, Iye, Akawo, Gowu, Kikgun, Aasa, Omu, Sanrin) — a ready-made, credited vocabulary of specific nouns for captions.
- **Zaria Art Society and "natural synthesis"** — founded **1958** by students at the Nigeria College of Arts, Science and Technology, Zaria (now Ahmadu Bello University), who "opposed the imposition of European art school ideas" and "adopted and promoted local visual art traditions like Nok sculptures and the Uli painting. The style of art promoted by the Zaria Art Society is now called **natural synthesis**." Named members: Jimoh Akolo, Yusuf Grillo, Demas Nwoko, Uche Okeke, Bruce Onobrakpeya, Simon Okeke, Oseloka Osadebe, Solomon Wangboje, Ogbonaya Nwagbara, Emmanuel Odita. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaria_Art_Society
  - *Why it matters*: SKU §11 lists the Zaria Art Society as an unverified research lead. **It is verified, and the name of its programme — "natural synthesis" — is arguably the best available description of what ARCHIVE's own identity should do.** The 1958 date also gives ARCHIVE a legitimate 1958–2026 span for a timeline.
- **Uli and the Nsukka School** — per Uche Okeke's article: "Ancient Nigerian symbols are most commonly found on pottery or in body paintings. Igbo designs are called Uli Patterns"; traditionally "a Uli artist is a female person in Igbo society who paints patterns on the body and sometimes on the walls of sacred places", a process called *Ide Uli* or *Ise Uli*. Critically: **"The motifs and symbols in Uli usually remain consistent however, it is the ordering of the design elements that challenge the ingenuity of traditional artists."** Okeke joined the Department of Fine and Applied Arts at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka in **1971**, and with Chike Aniakor and Obiora Udechukwu "established the formation of art and aesthetics of Nigerian modernism in the seventies" — the **Nsukka School**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uche_Okeke
  - *Why it matters*: this sentence is the theoretical basis for recommended direction R1 (§14). A fixed vocabulary whose **ordering** is the creative act is exactly a wordmark-with-states.
  - *Note:* there is no Wikipedia article at `Uli_(art)` (404). Cite Okeke's article for uli, not a standalone page.
- **Mbari Club** — "a centre for cultural activity by African writers, artists and musicians that was founded in **Ibadan, Nigeria, in 1961** by Ulli Beier, with the involvement of a group of young writers including Wole Soyinka and Chinua Achebe." The name was suggested by Achebe: *Mbari*, "an Igbo concept related to 'creation'". Other members included Christopher Okigbo, J. P. Clark, Ezekiel Mphahlele, Amos Tutuola. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mbari_Club
  - *Why it matters*: this is the **institutional ancestor** ARCHIVE should cite — a Nigerian cultural institution that was contemporary, cross-disciplinary (literature + art + music, like ARCHIVE's four disciplines) and explicitly modern.

**Sourcing caution.** All of the above come from Wikipedia articles. They are
reliable enough to *direct the design*, but SKU §8 and §11 require credible
sources credited **on the page**. Before any of these facts ship, each should be
re-verified against a primary or institutional source (Duke University Press for
Nka, the National Commission for Museums and Monuments, university presses, the
Smithsonian).

## 13. Brand-fit analysis

### 13.1 What the categories say about this brand

| Category | Verdict for ARCHIVE | Reasoning against SKU §6 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Typographic / wordmark** | **Primary.** | The positioning is literally "a cultural institution that behaves like a contemporary publication." Publications are wordmarks. Twelve of twelve institutional references here are type-led, and the two strongest (MoMA, Tate) hold one typographic asset for decades. |
| **Archival & editorial mastheads** | **Primary.** | This is where "curatorial and contemporary, not startup or agency" is already solved — The Gentlewoman, Aperture, The Republic, Nka. No symbol anywhere, no agency flex, full institutional weight. |
| **Minimal / editorial** | **Primary support.** | The antidote to the beige/serif/card-grid template is restraint plus one strong typographic move, not a pictorial device (National Gallery of Canada, Toledo, Baltimore). |
| **Industry-specific (African & diaspora)** | **Primary support.** | MOWAA, Studio Museum in Harlem, CAAM, Hutchins and Abbe Museum all show that a specific cultural institution can be designed in contemporary form. Abbe Museum specifically shows how to cite a craft tradition typographically instead of as pattern. |
| **Geometric** | **Secondary.** | Legitimate and available (Zeitz MOCAA, MoN Takanawa), but on this brand a geometric mark must be justified by *something real* — usually the institution's own building. ARCHIVE has no building. Treat as optional. |
| **Symbol / mark** | **Secondary, likely unnecessary.** | Only 2 of 6 entries here are "strong", and both are strong *because they refuse a symbol* (Serpentine's credited type identity; the Museum of Ethnography abstracting its own architecture). A standalone mark adds risk of cliché without adding meaning. |
| **Monogram** | **Weak.** | A monogram needs a short, recognisable initial set. "ARCHIVE" gives you a single letter that is claimed by thousands of brands. Not a differentiator. |
| **Expressive / experimental** | **Support, tightly scoped.** | SKU §7 requires motion, and the *stateful wordmark* route (R1) is expressive by construction. But festival identities (OFFF, Pohoda) sit in the wrong register; only Sónar's systematic generation and *Dancing Before the Moon*'s diaspora framing are transferable. |
| **Anti-patterns** | **Exclude, by name.** | All seven collide with SKU §6 or §10. |

### 13.2 The tension the identity has to resolve

SKU §3 states it exactly: *"the audience is young, fast and visual; the subject is
archival, historical and text-heavy."* The identity must therefore work at two
speeds:

1. **Instant** — a wordmark that states the institution in under a second, at any
   size, in one colour.
2. **Slow** — a system that rewards attention, because the content is archives.

The finding that resolves it: **one invariant, many states**. Tate proves the
formula in branding (Wolff Olins, 2000, four registered wordmark states); Uli
practice proves it in Nigerian design history ("the motifs and symbols in Uli
usually remain consistent however, it is the ordering of the design elements that
challenge the ingenuity of traditional artists"). The identity can be
simultaneously fast (the fixed state, instantly legible) and slow (the progression
through states, which *is* the timeline), and it can be justified from Nigerian
sources rather than borrowed from European branding practice.

### 13.3 Explicit non-recommendations

For the avoidance of doubt, and to protect against the "must avoid" list:

- **Do not** build the identity around a symbol. Not a globe, not a continent,
  not a mask, not an abstract "archive box". None survived scrutiny here.
- **Do not** invent an "African" pattern. Nsibidi and adire exist, are documented
  and are attributable; an invented pattern is indefensible.
- **Do not** use green-white-green, anywhere, for any reason.
- **Do not** adopt an agency/portfolio register — no oversized monogram, no
  gradient mesh, no "brandmark + wordmark stacked in a circle".
- **Do not** default to beige/serif/grid-of-cards and call it "archival". Archival
  is a *system* property, not a colour.

## 14. Recommended directions for the creative-direction agent

Ordered by strength of justification. Each names its precedents, so the
recommendation is defensible on project relevance rather than taste (SKU §6).

### R1 — "Fixed invariant, variable ordering": one wordmark, a registered set of archival states
**Recommended primary direction.**

- **What it is.** A single ARCHIVE wordmark with three to five *registered* states
  (e.g. solid, halftone/dot-screen, out-of-focus, duotone, outlined), plus rules
  for how they transition. The wordmark is never redrawn; only its state changes.
- **Precedent.** Tate — one wordmark issued as standard/blurred/faded/halftone,
  designed by Wolff Olins in 2000, updated by North in 2016
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate).
- **Justification from the project, not from taste.** Uli practice — a real Igbo
  art form in which the vocabulary stays fixed and the *ordering* is the creative
  act (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uche_Okeke) — supplies a Nigerian principle
  for exactly this mechanic; and the Zaria Art Society's programme of "natural
  synthesis" (founded 1958,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaria_Art_Society) is the historical precedent
  for an institution synthesising Nigerian sources with modern form. This
  directly encodes the brief's guiding principle: the same wordmark, in its older
  and newer states, *is* "the past exerting influence on the present".
- **Why it is cheap.** SKU §8: one designer, ~10 days. One wordmark plus state
  rules is far cheaper than a symbol system plus a wordmark, and it produces the
  motion vocabulary §7 requires for free.
- **Failure mode to guard.** States must be *registered* (a defined, finite set),
  not arbitrary. Tate works because there are four, and they are named.

### R2 — "The typeface is an artefact": headline face justified from Nigerian print history
**Recommended companion to R1.**

- **What it is.** Choose or commission a display face for headlines and the
  wordmark that can be *cited* — drawn from or closely modelled on Nigerian print,
  poster, book and record-sleeve typography of the period the archive covers.
  Where nothing defensible exists, state the reference and credit it.
- **Precedent.** Cooper Hewitt — "a new graphic identity, wordmark, and new
  website" launched with the museum's renaming in June 2014, by Eddie Opara of
  Pentagram (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Hewitt,_Smithsonian_Design_Museum);
  MoMA — Ivan Chermayeff, 1964, Franklin Gothic No. 2, unchanged for over 60 years
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Gothic).
- **Justification.** The archive is the source material. An institution whose
  subject is Nigerian graphic and print history should derive its typography from
  that history — which is the same move Abbe Museum makes with Wabanaki
  weaving, reviewed as "Wove it or Weave it"
  (https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_abbe_museum_by_in_house_international.php).
- **Guard.** Do not claim a provenance you cannot source. SKU §2: "no invented
  facts presented as history" applies to the brand as much as to the content.

### R3 — "Masthead, not logo": if in doubt, no symbol at all
**Recommended register, whichever structural direction wins.**

- **What it is.** Treat the identity as a publication masthead: wordmark, a
  strapline, a volume/issue line, a strict grid, and nothing else.
- **Precedent.** The Republic (Nigerian; wordmark shipped as a single SVG asset —
  https://republic.com.ng/), The Gentlewoman (https://thegentlewoman.co.uk/),
  Aperture (https://aperture.org/), Magnum Photos
  (https://www.magnumphotos.com/), Nka
  (https://www.dukeupress.edu/nka). The New Yorker's masthead has held for a
  century because the *system around it* changes and the letters do not
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker).
- **Justification.** This is SKU §6's positioning line implemented literally, and
  it is the single most defensible answer to "wordmark matters more than symbol".
  It also minimises the risk of tripping any anti-pattern, because there is no
  device in which to trip.

### R4 — If a mark is required: one documented Nigerian signifier, credited
**Conditional. Only if the Project Manager decides a separable mark is needed.**

- **What it is.** A single sign drawn from **nsibidi**, with its meaning, its
  people ("developed by the Ejagham people in the cross river region of Nigeria",
  c. 400 AD – present) and its documented diaspora descendants (Anaforuana in
  Cuba, Veve in Haiti) credited on the page.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsibidi
- **Justification.** It is a real system with verified continuity from Nigeria to
  the Caribbean — a genuine, citable "past → present" argument, which is the
  brief's guiding principle. And `africa` appears on only 20 Brand New entries
  against 880 `monogram` entries: specificity is the uncrowded position.
- **Guard, absolutely binding.** Nsibidi signs carry meanings. A sign used as
  decoration is exactly the failure mode of §11.5. If the sign's meaning cannot be
  stated and sourced, do not use it.

### R5 — Motion as the third pillar: the state transition *is* the timeline
**Recommended, scoped to R1.**

- **What it is.** Scroll and hover drive a wordmark between registered states; the
  transition is the only motion the brand owns. Everything else is content.
- **Precedent.** Sónar 2025 — expressiveness delivered as a systematic generated
  identity rather than hand-drawn flourish
  (https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_identity_for_sonar_2025_by_vagrant_morbo_brand_makers_and_thru_motion.php);
  York Mediale — logo and motion graphics commissioned as one deliverable
  (https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_motion_graphics_for_york_mediale_by_something_more_and_hungry_sandwich_club.php).
- **Justification.** SKU §7 requires motion that "supports the story without
  hurting usability", and SKU §8 warns that motion is cheap to build and carries
  cultural weight. A single, meaningful, low-cost transition beats a portfolio of
  scroll effects — and keeps ARCHIVE out of the festival/agency register.

### R6 — Write the exclusion list into the brand system document
**Process recommendation.**

Copy §11 verbatim into `artifacts/04-brand/document/index.html` as a written
"excluded" clause with the schematics sheet alongside it. SKU §6's "must avoid"
list is only enforceable if the design has a place to check itself against.

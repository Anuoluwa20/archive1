**This is the creative premise, not a blocker.** Three consequences run through every direction below:

1. **Typography, colour, layout and motion must carry the entire cultural load.** This is not a compromise — it is the opportunity SKU §8 names ("Strong typographic identity is cheap to build and carries cultural weight"), and it is corroborated by `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §1: six of the ten strongest institutional references are wordmark-only systems with no symbol at all.
2. **Imagery must be handled with one of exactly three honest moves:** (a) a *labelled, measured field* where a work would be, carrying the real credit and the real rights status; (b) a *generative or diagrammatic* treatment made by us from our own data, labelled as a diagram; (c) *deliberate absence*, stated. 
3. **Never fake a photograph. Never let an illustration read as a historical record.** Distribution across the three directions: A leans on (a), B organises itself around (a)+(c), C needs (b) because the system is generative from the start.

## 2. DIRECTION A — "THE MASTHEAD"

### A1. The idea in one line

**ARCHIVE is published, not exhibited: a dark, type-led cultural publication whose masthead, folio line, issue number and credit columns do the work that photographs would normally do.**

### A2. What it says about the brand

SKU §6 states the positioning verbatim: *"a cultural institution that behaves like a contemporary publication — curatorial rigour, editorial confidence, modern craft."* Direction A is that sentence taken literally and built. It says: **this is a record you can cite, and a voice you can trust.** It borrows authority from the *magazine* shelf rather than the *museum* vitrine — which is precisely where `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1 locates the tone young Nigerian creatives already respond to ("the tone ARCHIVE wants lives in magazines"), and where it identifies the open position: *"dark, type-led, interaction-first — with world-class craft."*

It also inherits the single most defensible register in the identity research: **R3, "masthead, not logo."** `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §13.1 puts the Typographic/wordmark and Editorial-masthead categories as *Primary* for this brand, and §13.2 resolves the core tension (fast audience / slow subject) in favour of "one invariant, many states."

Crucially, A is also the direction that says the **least** it cannot prove. `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` §5 flags that **Design is thin** and §8C warns that presenting all four disciplines as equally evidenced would be overclaiming. A publication has a native, honest way to handle uneven evidence: **the credit line, the confidence marker, and the "starting point" note.** That is editorial practice, not an apology.

### A3. The feeling on first encounter

You land on near-black. One enormous line of type states the purpose. Under it, in small mono, a folio: `VOL. 01 · 1856–2026 · LAGOS`. There is no photograph. It feels like opening a serious issue — **confident, adult, unhurried, warm rather than austere.** Nothing is shouting. Nothing is asking you to book a visit. The page is clearly *edited* by someone.

By the second screen you understand the structure: four departments, each with its own treatment, plus one story spread.

**Not** the feeling of a museum homepage. **Not** the feeling of a design portfolio.

Do not budget for it now. |

#### A4.1 A real, specific typographic risk — and why it is a design feature

ARCHIVE's own subject matter contains Yoruba orthography: **adire** (*adi* "to tie" + *re* "to soak"), **aso oke / Aṣọ òkè / Òfì**, **jùjú**, **Àdìrẹ**. Yoruba needs both **tone marks** and **sub-dotted vowels (ẹ, ọ, ṣ)** stacked on the same letter. Most display faces do not carry them; a catalogue in Space Mono will show tofu or lose the marks.

**This is a specificity test the page either passes or fails in public.** Direction A's rule:

> **The mono layer is reserved for things that are not Yoruba** (dates, accession numbers, credits, dimensions). **Any Yoruba or Igbo term is set in Charis or Archivo, never in mono.** The typesetting spec names the script per role. Characters must be proofed before ship, not after.

This is the honest substitute for **R2** ("the typeface is an artefact… justified from Nigerian print history"). I could not verify the existence of a Nigerian-designed typeface suitable for this brief, and I will not invent one (§12). So R2 is satisfied in the two ways that are actually available: **(a)** the type must be *competent in Nigerian languages*, and **(b)** the type choice must be *stated and credited on the page*, exactly as Abbe Museum credits Wabanaki weaving typographically (`LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §10, reviewed as "Wove it or Weave it").

#### A4.2 Scale and usage

- Masthead is the largest type on the page, `clamp(3rem, 13vw, 12.5rem)`, weight 700, tracking `-0.03em`, and it **never moves**. It is the invariant.
- Section and discipline display: `clamp(2.75rem, 4vw + 1.5rem, 4.75rem)`, weight 800, tracking `-0.02em`, line-height `0.95`.
- Body: 17–19px, line-height 1.55, measure capped at **68ch**. This is the only direction of the three where long-form reading is a first-class requirement.
- Labels/index lines: 11–12px Space Mono, **uppercase, tracking `+0.14em`**. Uppercase is reserved for the catalogue voice only — never for body or headlines, which keeps the page out of "shouty institutional" register.
- Two-face discipline: **display = Archivo, document = Charis.** They never mix inside one line. (Precedent: Drents Museum's two-face typographic pairing, cited in `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §8 as evidence that a pairing reads institutional, not startup.)

**Hard rules.** No green, anywhere, for any reason — SKU §6's ban on "green-white-green shorthand" is treated as a ban on the hue in this system, so a green focus ring or a green success state is not available. No gradients. Discipline colour appears only as a marker rule and a label — **never** as a card fill, a section background, or a badge, which is how the "four coloured cards" cliché gets in.

### A6. Layout and grid logic

**The grid is a publication grid, and the page has a spine.**

- 12 columns, gutter `clamp(16px, 2vw, 32px)`, max content width `1440px`, outer margin `clamp(20px, 5vw, 96px)`.
- **Persistent left rail (the spine):** a fixed 1px rule with the folio metadata set vertically in mono — section number, section name, and the issue line. **This rail is also the navigation.** It is the single cheapest, most legible way to make a landing page feel published rather than assembled, and it doubles as a progress indicator with no extra machinery.
- **Asymmetry is deliberate.** Default text arrangement is a **7/5 split** (text/notations), not centred. Centred layouts are what make archives look like brochures.
- **Baseline grid** of 8px for all vertical rhythm; long-form copy always on the baseline.
- **Running heads and folios:** each section is numbered `01`–`08` matching SKU §5, printed in the rail and in the section opener — so the reader always knows where in the issue they are.
- **One full-bleed moment per section, never more.** Restraint is what separates an editorial page from a poster.

**The three non-grid moments** (one per required "memorable moment", per `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §5.7):

1. **Featured Archive = a spread, not a grid.** Two facing fields, recto/verso, with the serendipity mechanic from `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §3 — *two works linked by one keyword*, with a roll/refresh affordance. It is keyboard-operable, cheap, and it *enacts* "the past's influence on the present" by joining two objects decades apart under one idea. The keyword is the material; the pairing is the argument.
2. **Stories Through Time = a contents spread.** Not a card row. A horizontal, typographically-set contents list — `1968 — J.D. 'Okhai Ojeikere begins the hairstyles project` — with the four disciplines as four hanging indents. Time is the spine of the list, exactly as `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §4 recommends.
3. **Closing = a colophon.** The credits, sources and rights statements are set as the *final spread of the issue*, not as small print in the footer. SKU §8 makes real credits a non-negotiable; A makes them the closing statement. That is the strongest possible expression of "sourcing reads as respect" (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §4.3).

### A7. How the four disciplines are treated distinctly

Each discipline gets a **different publication furniture**, never a different colour card:

| Discipline | Treatment | Why it fits the verified record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photography** | **The plate section.** A measured field at the true aspect ratio of the absent work, with a caption block beneath: *title, date, series, holding institution, accession, rights status.* The field contains the work's **name**, not an image. | Ojeikere's hairstyles project has a start date (1968), a scale (≈1,000 prints; 10,000+ photographs) and **four verified institutional holdings** — so the caption can be completely specific without a single pixel of the photograph. |
| **Music** | **Side A / Side B listing.** Numbered rows in mono: an artist, a year, a lineage. Side A is the historical figure; Side B is what descends from them. | The verified music chain is unusually clean: palm-wine → highlife (Ghanaian origin, **Igbo highlife** Nigerian, Nigeria's most popular genre in the 1960s) → jùjú → fuji → Afrobeat → Afrobeats. The page can distinguish **Afrobeat from Afrobeats** — which `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` §8B flags as a live trap *and* "a good story beat." |
| **Fashion** | **The spec sheet.** A garment-specification table: object, date, designer, material, holding, accession. | Shade Thomas-Fahm's V&A record is object-level verified: 'Princess Kaftan' (1970s), accession **T.5-2020**, credit "Given by Shade Thomas-Fahm", "Embroidered of aṣọ-òkè fu cotton" (`https://api.vam.ac.uk/v2/museumobject/O1616494`). A spec sheet is the *only* honest way to present an object you cannot show — and it happens to look like a couture tag. |
| **Design** | **The colophon / poster page.** Oversized type used as poster, set against a flat colophon block listing movements, actors and dates. | The register is available because the design history *is* print history: the Zaria Art Society (1958–1962, "natural synthesis", Uche Okeke's term), the Mbari Club as a publisher (Ibadan, 1961; 17 titles in the 1960s), and Emokpae's FESTAC emblem (1973). |

**Why this holds structurally.** Four different furniture types, one masthead, one grid, one credit convention, four markers. The reader learns "Photography is a plate section, Music is a setlist" — a *legible* structure, which is what "explore the disciplines easily" (SKU §9) actually requires. `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §5.6 is explicit that **"one shared grid for all four is the single fastest way to look generic"** — A answers that without needing four palettes.

### A8. How motion serves "the past becoming present"

**Motion is typesetting, not choreography.** The claim A makes is that a thing can be the same thing in a different state — so the motion vocabulary is *state change on fixed structures*.

1. **Registered wordmark states (R1, R5).** One invariant masthead, four named states. Nothing is ever redrawn; only the state changes.
   - `01 SOLID` — default; also the print, `prefers-reduced-motion`, and 16px state.
   - `02 SCREEN` — a dot/halftone fill, the "printed record" state. Used across the Featured Archive.
   - `03 SOFT` — out-of-focus, the "attributed / uncertain / memory" state.
   - `04 OUTLINE` — the "lead / not yet verified" state.
   The masthead advances one state per major section as you scroll. **The same letters, older and newer, is literally "the past exerting influence on the present."** The justification is not taste: Uli practice is a real Igbo art form in which *"The motifs and symbols in Uli usually remain consistent however, it is the ordering of the design elements that challenge the ingenuity of traditional artists"* (`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uche_Okeke`), and Tate is the branding precedent for a registered family of wordmark states (Wolff Olins, 2000 — `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate`).
2. **The printing reveal.** Section openers wipe in as a press impression: a 2px clip-path wipe at `420ms`, once, never re-triggered on scroll-back. Guard: `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §14 R1's **"failure mode to guard" — states must be registered and finite, not arbitrary.** Four named states, four moments. Not a scroll-driven free-for-all.
3. **Hover = a rule, never a scale.** Discipline markers extend their 1px rule by 12px on hover/focus. That is the entire interaction vocabulary for cards. It is fast, it is accessible, and it resists the agency register.

**The honesty system — A's most important contribution.** `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` carries a **Confidence** column on every row (High / Medium–High / Medium / Low / Attributed / Not verified). A turns that editorial rule into a **visual, unmistakeable system**:

| Confidence | Marker | Rendering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High** (often institution-corroborated) | `●` solid | Line, no hedging. |
| **Medium / Medium–High** | `◐` screen (dot) | Same type, screened marker. |
| **Attributed / Low** | `○` outline | Copy must read "described by [source] as…" |
| **Unverified** | `⊘` struck | **Not on the page.** Shown only in the excluded appendix. |

This means the design **cannot** overclaim: the marker is generated from the register, not from a designer's judgement at build time. It answers `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` §8C (design is thin; four disciplines are not equally documented) and it makes SKU §8's "label anything uncertain" structural rather than an afterthought. Leading candidate for the page's second-strongest memorable device after the wordmark.

### A9. How imagery is handled under the no-archive rule

Four moves, in priority order:

1. **Measured absence.** Where a work belongs, a field at its true aspect ratio, with the work's name, date, holding institution, accession and **rights status printed in the field**. The absence is *stated*, not styled.
2. **The single cleared image.** The Met's public-domain image of the Benin ivory mask of Queen Idia, used in the Design department — **never decoratively cropped**, and captioned with the full credit and with its history named: looted from the palace of the Oba of Benin in the 1897 Benin Expedition, and the source object for Emokpae's 1973 FESTAC emblem. `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` §7 is explicit that "if the page uses it, that history has to be named."
3. **Our own diagrams, labelled as diagrams.** Anything generative is captioned "[diagram — made for this page]" so it can never be mistaken for a record.
4. **Nothing else.** No stock, no AI-generated "heritage" imagery, no faux-vintage processing.

### A10. Strengths

- The **most defensible** of the three: it is SKU §6's own positioning sentence, implemented, backed by the strongest category evidence in `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` (§13.1: typographic + editorial = Primary).
- The **most buildable** in 10 days: no custom illustration, no generative system, no horizontal scrub. Type, a grid, one inversion, four states.
- The **best long-form readability and accessibility** profile — high contrast by construction, a real measure, real headings, real tabular data.
- The **best degradation path**: with JS off, motion reduced, fonts blocked to system stacks, or mid-load on a 3G connection, A still looks like a quiet, deliberate publication. Nothing "breaks" because nothing depends on a mechanism.
- It gives **credits a place of honour**, which SKU §8 makes non-negotiable.

### A11. Weaknesses and risks

- **The beige/serif trap.** Charis is a serif; the whole direction dies if the serif takes over the page. *Mitigation:* the masthead and all display are grotesque; Charis is restricted to running text and quoted material; and Newsprint appears exactly once.
- **It can read as "just a nice editorial site."** That is the real risk: indistinguishable-from-good-taste. *Mitigation:* the honesty system (A8) and the plate sections (A7) are the parts nobody else has. If they get cut for time, the direction loses its argument.
- **Comparison risk to The Republic / nataal / MOWAA.** `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §4.3 establishes that these sites win on issues/editions and full-bleed imagery. A has the issue framing but reads with **fewer images than any of them** — so A must out-edit them, not out-photograph them.

### A12. What could make it fail

- **Weak copy.** A publication with bad headlines is a bad publication. There is no visual spectacle to hide behind. If the writing is not specific — named works, named people, real dates — A collapses to a template.
- **An undisciplined grid.** If the asymmetry drifts into arbitrary full-bleed moments, A becomes a blog.
- **Unlabelled absence.** If the fields aren't honestly captioned, the page looks unfinished or, worse, looks like it lost its images.
- **The honesty markers misused** — if a Medium-confidence claim is set solid `●`, the whole system's credibility is gone and the page has broken SKU §8.
- **Scope greed.** Eight sections plus four distinct treatments is already a lot for one person. Cutting to the SKU §5 minimum viable page is the right call, not a compromise.

## 3. DIRECTION B — "THE ABSENT OBJECT"

### B1. The idea in one line

**The page is a room, and it is an exhibition designed around absence: every photograph ARCHIVE cannot show is present as a labelled, measured void, at the exact size the object would occupy.**

### B2. What it says about the brand

It says: **we will not lie to you, and we will not decorate our way around what we don't have.** Where A handles the rights problem editorially, B makes it the *exhibition's subject* — an institution that says "this photograph exists, it is held at the Museum of Modern Art, it is not ours, and here is what is in it."

That is a stance with real moral force behind it, because the register's facts are unusually stark: Ojeikere's archive is ~10,000 photographs, the hairstyles project began in 1968 and produced ~1,000 prints, and **not one of them can be reproduced here** (Met and AIC both `isPublicDomain: false`; MoMA and the V&A route reproduction through licensing). A page that *names* that gap is doing something a page that scrolls past it is not.

Its identity precedent is the most instructive note in the whole research document — **Museum of Ethnography, Budapest**, cited in `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §5 and §11.7: the correct answer for a museum holding other people's material culture is to **abstract the institution's own building, not the cultures it holds**, and never to present material as specimen. B is the direction that takes that instruction most seriously: the *room* is the subject, and the objects are treated as absent-but-named rather than as display.

### B3. The feeling on first encounter

A large dark room. Warm black, not cold. One line of type, larger than comfortable. Then space — a lot of it. At the edge of the emptiness, small and precise, a wall label with a measured outline around nothing.

You feel the gap before you understand it. It is atmospheric, quiet, un-commercial — closer to walking into a gallery before the lights warm up than to opening a magazine. There is a wayfinding number to your left. You can see a floor plan.

**It does not feel like a website.** That is the point, and it is also the risk.

### B4. Typography

| Role | Face | Licence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Display / wall titles** | **Fraunces** (Undercase Type) | **SIL OFL 1.1** — verified at `https://github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces` | A "display 'Old Style' soft-serif… inspired by the mannerisms of early 20th century typefaces such as Windsor, Souvenir, and the Cooper Series", with variable axes **opsz / wght / SOFT / WONK**. That is the key: it can be tuned to sit *straight and serious* rather than friendly-retro. |
| **Wall labels + UI** | **Inter** | **SIL OFL 1.1** | Small-size legibility, tabular figures, case-sensitive forms — correct for a label spec at 12–14px. |
| **Measurements, dimensions, accession numbers** | **Space Mono** | **SIL OFL 1.1** | The catalogue voice. Latin Extended only — **Yoruba terms must not be set in this layer** (same rule as A4.1). |
| **Long-form / any Nigerian-language term** | **Charis** | **SIL OFL** | Diacritic competence. Non-negotiable anywhere `Àdìrẹ`/`Aṣọ òkè`/`Òfì` appear. |
| *Commercial upgrade* | **GT Alpina** (Grilli Type; designed by **Reto Moser, released 2020**, 70 styles) | **Commercial** — verified at `https://www.grillitype.com/typefaces/gt-alpina` | "A workhorse serif" that "reaches into the grab bag of typographic history." Its published language support list **includes Igbo**. Grilli Type's own "in use" page shows it on an exhibition ("Wes Anderson: Edge Of The Frame") and on a poster project ("Radical Utopias") — i.e. verified in exactly this register. |

**Fraunces discipline — this is where B could go wrong.** Fraunces derives partly from the **Cooper Series**, the archetypal friendly retro poster face. Used loosely, B becomes a 1970s revival. The spec: **`WONK 0`, `SOFT 0`, `opsz` at display maximum, weight 300–400, not 700+.** Set straight, wide-tracked and quiet, Fraunces reads as a serious large-optical-size serif. **Charis or Inter carry all body text; Fraunces never sets a paragraph.**

**The deliberate decision: colour is architectural, not categorical.** B does **not** give the four disciplines four colours. **There is one accent for the whole building, and the rooms are distinguished by number, scale and treatment.** Four category colours is exactly how the "colour-coded card grid" museum template happens; refusing it is a stated design position (and it is the reasoning behind `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §5.6, which warns that one shared treatment is the fastest route to generic).

No green. No gradients.

### B6. Layout and grid logic

**The layout is a plan, not a page.**

- **Full-viewport rooms.** Each of the four disciplines occupies a room it does not share. Sections are entered, not scrolled past.
- **Sticky section labels** act as wayfinding, positioned like wall signage at a consistent edge — not floating badges.
- **Spatial numbering `01`–`04`** in mono at large scale, printed where a room number goes.
- **A floor-plan index** in the nav: the four disciplines as rooms off a corridor, with the featured archive as a central vitrine.
- **The absence is measured.** A void is drawn to the true aspect ratio of the work it stands for, with **dimension lines and millimetre-style figures** in mono at its base, exactly as a technical drawing annotates a part. This is the one place B asks the reader to do work, and it is the whole idea.
- **Generous, aggressive negative space.** Whitespace is the primary material. Anything that fills it is argued for.
- **Labels sit beside the object, not centred** — because that is where real labels are, and because centred captions immediately look like a template.

### B7. How the four disciplines are treated distinctly

| Discipline | Treatment | Verified substance it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photography** | **The plate room.** Voids at true aspect ratio; dimension lines; a label with holding institution and rights status. | Four verified holders for Ojeikere alone (MoMA, the Met, AIC, the V&A) plus the 2013 Venice Biennale Arsenale inclusion. The labels can be entirely factual and still carry the room. |
| **Music** | **The listening room.** No images are needed at all — the room is a *programme*. A typographic field built from FESTAC '77's real figures (15 Jan – 12 Feb 1977; ~16,000 participants; 56 African nations and diaspora countries; ~500,000 spectators) as a set of spatial typographic marks. | FESTAC carries all four disciplines at once and is High-confidence across the register. |
| **Fashion** | **The handling table.** Garment spec labels beside *drawn textile diagrams* — a resist-dye field, a weave diagram — rendered as pure geometry and captioned **"[diagram, not a photograph]"**. | V&A object data: 'Princess Kaftan', T.5-2020; adire records; aso oke 1876–1899 pieces and a 2021 Kenneth Ize piece. |
| **Design** | **The poster wall.** The largest type on the page, and **the one real photograph** — the Met's public-domain Idia mask — with the 1897 loot history named in its label, and the FESTAC emblem lineage (Emokpae, 1973) laid beside it. | `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_ivory_mask` · `https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/318622` · `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhabor_Emokpae` |

### B8. How motion serves "the past becoming present"

Motion here is **lighting and footfall** — environmental, slow, low-amplitude.

- **Rooms change light.** Crossing a room threshold cross-fades the wall: a 720ms opacity/background transition. Nothing slides.
- **The void resolves into type.** On entry, the measured outline draws first (a 420ms stroke dash), then the *label* fills in. **The past becomes present by becoming legible, not by becoming pictured.** This is the direction's central motion idea and it is a genuinely original reading of the brief's guiding principle.
- **Wayfinding numerals switch** as rooms change — a hard 240ms swap, no animation curve, because signage is discrete.
- **A slow parallax of the section label only** — maximum 12px of travel. Not the content. Ever.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` produces a static wall**, which is a complete and deliberately good design, not a degraded one.

### B9. How imagery is handled under the no-archive rule

**B is the direction whose entire form *is* the answer to this question.** Absence is drawn, measured, labelled and named. One genuine public-domain photograph appears, once, with its credit, its rights status and its history. Everything else is either a void or a diagram captioned as a diagram. There is no fourth category, which means there is no place for a fake.

### B10. Strengths

- **The most distinctive and the most memorable** of the three. No Nigerian cultural-archive experience is built this way, and the position is genuinely unoccupied (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2D: no Nigerian museum or cultural-archive site appears in Awwwards' Nigeria listing).
- **The best fit for the brief's word "immersive."** SKU §1 asks for "an immersive cultural destination"; SKU §5 proposes a "digital exhibition." B is the only direction that is *spatially* immersive rather than editorially immersive.
- **The strongest answer to "make people feel its influence."** Naming what is missing, precisely, is more affecting than showing stock.
- **Zero imagery risk.** It cannot accidentally misuse a right.
- **It gives the Serpentine/Museum-of-Ethnography discipline on-brand**: the identity abstracts the *institution's own space*, which is the correct move for an institution holding material it does not own.

### B11. Weaknesses and risks

- **It centres the limitation, not the work.** The message risks becoming "we don't have the pictures" rather than "look what Nigeria made." The brief's guiding principle is about the *past's influence*, not about an institution's licensing constraints.
- **It depends on caption craft at a level the plan does not budget for.** Every void carries the entire page. That is a copywriting project, and SKU §8 assigns one designer/developer.
- **A void can read as a bug.** `CONTENT_REGISTER.md` §7 means there is no fallback if a reader decides the page is broken.
- **Weakest degradation path.** With JS off or an image/CSS failure, B looks like a failed render. A looks quiet. C looks marked-up.
- **Highest risk of the SKU §6 agency/portfolio exclusion** — conceptual exhibition design is exactly what studios put on Awwwards, and B would have to work hard not to look like a studio's own art project.
- **The scale is expensive in space.** Full-viewport rooms plus measured voids across eight sections is a lot of vertical page for a landing page whose job is to be scanned.

### B12. What could make it fail

- **Reading as empty rather than as deliberate** — the single most likely failure, and it is fatal, because the audience the brief prioritises is "young, fast and visual."
- **Losing nerve and filling the voids** with an AI-generated or stock image at the last minute. That destroys the direction's whole ethical position and would breach SKU §8.
- **The Fraunces tuning drifting retro**, tipping the page into 1970s-poster pastiche and into the "faux-nostalgic heritage" register.
- **No visible floor plan / wayfinding**, in which case the rooms are just long sections and B becomes A with more whitespace.
- **One image used twice, or used decoratively.** The stance is all-or-nothing.

## 4. DIRECTION C — "THE SIGNAL"

### C1. The idea in one line

**ARCHIVE as a transmission: a fixed vocabulary of marks whose constant re-ordering across a real 1856–2026 time axis *is* the design, so the page enacts continuity rather than illustrating it.**

### C2. What it says about the brand

It says: **Nigerian creative history did not stop, and did not stay still — it is one signal, repeatedly re-encoded.** It is the most assertive of the three positions, and it has the strongest single piece of verified support in the entire research corpus:

> **Nsibidi** is "a system of symbols or proto-writing developed by the Ejagham people in the cross river region of Nigeria and south-western pa[rt]", period "**circa 400 AD – present**", with documented descendant systems **Anaforuana (Cuba)** and **Veve (Haiti)**. — `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsibidi`

That is a **Nigerian sign system with a documented, citable Caribbean continuity**. It is a real "past → present" story that can be *cited rather than asserted* — which is precisely the brief's guiding principle, and it is why `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §12 calls it "the strongest single find of the research."

And the design *mechanic* has the same shape: Uli practice, where *"The motifs and symbols in Uli usually remain consistent however, it is the ordering of the design elements that challenge the ingenuity of traditional artists"* (`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uche_Okeke`). A fixed vocabulary, re-ordered — that is a documented Nigerian design principle, and it is also exactly how a generative identity works.

### C3. The feeling on first encounter

Movement, immediately. A field of small marks that assembles, disperses and re-assembles as you scroll. Behind it, a long horizontal axis running from `1856` to `2026`, with four parallel lanes for the four disciplines. Type that resolves from a dot-screen into solid, and back.

It feels like an instrument rather than a page. It feels *current* in the way `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §4.3 describes currency: an issue framing, a mechanism you can operate, and a visible time structure. It is the loudest of the three, and the most likely to be screenshotted.

### C4. Typography

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Display / axis titles** | **Archivo** | **SIL OFL 1.1** | Not for taste — for the **width axis**: ExtraCondensed→Expanded lets the same word become a system, compressing into a timeline and expanding at a section head. Its Latin Extended Additional coverage is also what allows Yoruba sub-dotted vowels (see A4.1). |
| **Secondary display / mechanical voice** | **Space Grotesk** (Florian Karsten, 2018 — a proportional variant of Space Mono) | **SIL OFL 1.1** — verified at `https://github.com/floriankarsten/space-grotesk` | "Retains the monospace's idiosyncratic details while optimizing for improved readability at non-display sizes" — old-style and tabular figures, stylistic alternates. The mechanical register, at reading size. |

| Token | Hex | Name | Usage rule |

| `--era-1` | `#1A1712` | **1856–1959 · Plate** | First temporal register. |

| `--scroll-ink` | *derived* | **Screen density** | The system's real colour: the same ink at 100% / 60% / 30% / 12% **dot-screen densities**. |

**The position: colour is density, not hue.** The identity's colour system is *halftone density on one ink* — the wordmark's states, the markers, and the timeline's eras are all the same ink at different dot densities. That is: you can build the entire colour system out of one colour, which is both a rigorous idea and a performance gift. The three era registers are **discrete, hard-boundaried, and applied at named dates** — never a continuous gradient. A gradient time bar is decoration and is banned by this direction's own rules.

No green. No gradients.

### C6. Layout and grid logic

**Time is the axis. This is the direction's whole structural argument.**

- **A single horizontal scrub** runs the length of "Stories Through Time", `1856 → 2026`, with **four lanes** — one per discipline — so the reader sees FESTAC '77, Ojeikere's 1968, the Zaria Art Society's 1958 and the Mbari Club's 1961 *lining up vertically*. The four-discipline structure becomes visible rather than declared.
- **Marker system, not colour coding.** The four disciplines are marked by **four distinct mark shapes**, drawn as pure geometry. Nothing is invented and passed off as documented: the marks are presented as a **proposed marker vocabulary**, and the direction's binding rule is that if a mark is drawn from Nsibidi, **its meaning, its people and its diaspora descendants must be stated and sourced on the page, or the mark is not used** (`LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §14 R4, guard "absolutely binding"; anti-pattern §11.5).
- **Vertical fallback is the default, not the fallback.** Mobile and `prefers-reduced-motion` get a vertical axis with a sticky rail. The horizontal scrub is an enhancement for pointer devices only.
- **Non-grid moments:** (1) the horizontal timeline; (2) the **transmission line** — the serendipity mechanic from `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §3, drawn as an actual line connecting two points on the axis, "two works linked by one keyword"; (3) the marker field resolving at the closing section.

### C7. How the four disciplines are treated distinctly

| Discipline | Treatment | Verified substance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photography** | **The contact strip.** A lane of measured empty frames, sequence as the content, density encoding era. | The one discipline with a dense chronology: 1856 (Daniel West), from 1880 (Lagos studios), 1890s–1910 (Neils Walwin Holm), 1930s (Alonge), 1947 (NPPA), 1950s–60s boom, 1968 (Ojeikere), 2013 (Venice). |
| **Music** | **The frequency lane.** Waves drawn as pure SVG geometry **from our own data** — dates, participant counts, album counts — captioned as diagrams. | FESTAC's 16,000 participants / 56 nations; Barrister's 70+ albums from a 1965 break; Adé's 1967 band → 1982 Island Records. |

| **Design** | **The grid lane.** Poster geometry; the FESTAC emblem's lineage; "natural synthesis" stated as a method rather than a mood. | Zaria 1958–1962 and Okeke's coinage "natural synthesis" (`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaria_Art_Society`); the Mbari Club's 17 titles (1961–). |

### C8. How motion serves "the past becoming present"

**In C, motion is not a device — it is the brand.** `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §14 R5 is implemented as fully as it can be.

- **The marker field re-orders on scroll.** The same marks, in new arrangements — the Uli principle rendered as a runtime behaviour. This is a direct, citable justification for a scroll-driven system, and almost nobody in this category has one.
- **The wordmark's dot-screen density interpolates at era boundaries.** The past is literally lower-resolution; the present is solid. This is a legibility argument *and* an emotional one, and it is cheap: a CSS mask or a single SVG `<pattern>` with an animated transform.
- **The axis scrubs** — the reader drags time. Guarded: keyboard-operable (arrow keys), a visible focus ring, no scroll-jacking, and never the primary scrolling mechanism.
- **Guardrails, all binding:** at most **three** concurrent animated properties; one `IntersectionObserver` and one `requestAnimationFrame` loop; durations 120–1200ms; `prefers-reduced-motion` pins everything to `state 01 SOLID` and a static vertical axis; `will-change` never applied to more than 3 nodes at once.

### C9. How imagery is handled under the no-archive rule

**C barely notices the rule, because the system is generative from the start.** Every visual is drawn from our own data or our own geometry, and captioned as a diagram. There are no voids to fill and no absences to explain. The one available photograph (the Met's Idia mask) may appear on the Design lane with full credit and its loot history named — or not at all.

The **reverse** risk is C's real one: a system that generates marks can accidentally *look* like it is documenting something it is not. The binding rule is therefore the reverse of A and B: **any mark that carries a meaning must have a stated source, or it is captioned `[formal mark — no documented meaning claimed]`.**

### C10. Strengths

- **The most distinctive**, and the only one of the three that is memorable *as a system* rather than as a page.
- **Motion-native**, which is what SKU §7 asks for and what `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §5.11 warns must be "deliberate and few."
- **The best structural answer to "Stories Through Time."** It makes chronology the layout rather than a section, which is the exact lesson `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §3 draws from `harvardfilmarchive.org` (Awwwards SOTD 2019, Huncwot).
- **The strongest "past → present" argument** available, because it can cite nsibidi's documented continuity from the Cross River region to Cuba and Haiti rather than asserting a connection.
- **No imagery problem at all** — the cleanest rights story of the three.

### C11. Weaknesses and risks

- **Build risk is the highest by a wide margin.** A horizontal scrub, a generative marker system, four lanes and era-interpolating wordmark states is not a 10-day solo job on top of eight content sections.
- **Performance risk.** A scroll-driven generative system on top of a landing page is exactly how a page misses SKU §10's performance mitigation.
- **Accessibility risk.** Horizontal scroll, scrub interactions, motion-dependent meaning and low-density halftone text are all real hazards. SKU §7 requires keyboard-accessible controls; a scrub is hard to make genuinely keyboard-equivalent.
- **The cultural risk, and it is the serious one.** Nsibidi is a **system of signs that carry meanings**, developed by the Ejagham people, still in use, with living descendants in Cuba and Haiti. Using such a system as UI furniture is *precisely* the failure mode `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §11.5 catalogues for invented tribal marks — and, worse, it would do so with a *real* system, which is a greater offence than inventing one. Every single mark would need its meaning sourced individually: **a research dependency that cannot be closed inside this stage's budget.**
- **Register risk.** Motion-led generative identity is the festival/agency register that SKU §6 explicitly excludes. Only Sónar 2025 and *Dancing Before the Moon* survive that test in the research (`LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §9).

### C12. What could make it fail

- **Shipping it 70% built.** A generative system at 70% looks broken; a publication at 70% still looks like a publication. This is C's fatal risk given the 10-day budget.
- **Any sign used decoratively.** Instantly breaches §11.5.
- **Motion on the critical path** — jank on a mid-range Android in Lagos, which is the audience, not an edge case.
- **The scrub hijacking scroll**, which would breach SKU §7's usability requirement outright.
- **The timeline becoming a gradient strip** — beautiful, meaningless decoration, and the exact trap the direction's own colour rules exist to prevent.

## 6. Recommendation

### 6.1 I recommend **Direction A — "The Masthead"**, with two imports

Take **A** as the build. Then import exactly two things from the others:

1. **From B: the absence policy.** Titled, dated, measured, institutionally-attributed, rights-statused fields where images belong — with dimension-labelled scale. A should *not* adopt B's full-viewport rooms or its "absence as subject" framing. It should adopt B's **honesty clause**: never a silent empty box, always a named one.
2. **From C: the registered-state mechanics and the marker system.** C's best idea is not the horizontal scrub — it is that **one invariant re-ordered across time is the identity**. That is `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` R1, and it lives naturally inside A as four named masthead states plus the four discipline markers and the four confidence markers. A gets C's best contribution without C's build and cultural risk.

### 6.2 Why A, honestly

**Because A is the only direction that wins on both halves of the same decision.** SKU §9 asks for two things that the other two directions trade against each other: a *distinctive, culturally grounded* direction, and a page that *works*, is *usable*, and is *built by one person in ten days*. B and C buy distinctiveness with delivery risk and accessibility debt. A buys it with editorial system — which is cheap, robust, and degrades gracefully.

**Because it is the positioning, not an interpretation of it.** SKU §6 says the institution should behave "like a contemporary publication." `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1 independently confirms that the tone this audience trusts "lives in magazines," and that the unoccupied position is *"dark, type-led, interaction-first — with world-class craft."* A is not a stylistic preference; it is the research's own conclusion, implemented.

**Because it is the only direction that turns SKU §8 into an asset rather than a workaround.** The rights constraint is *the* structural problem in this project, and A is the only one of the three that converts it into a design feature (named, attributed absences) rather than a subject (B) or an irrelevance (C). It also converts `CONTENT_REGISTER.md`'s Confidence column into a visual system — an original contribution that makes "no invented facts" and "label anything uncertain" enforceable by the design itself, rather than by a reviewer's memory.

**Because it survives being unfinished.** With 10 days and one person, the probability of shipping at ~75% is high. At 75%, A is a quiet, deliberate publication; B is an exhibition that looks incomplete; C is a system that looks broken. Choosing the direction with the best partial state is not pessimism — it is the correct read of SKU §8.

**Because its biggest weakness is fixable and the others' are not.** A's weakness is "it can read as just a nice editorial site," and the fix is specific and inside the budget: the plate sections and the honesty markers. B's weakness is that it centres the institution's limitation; C's is that it depends on sourcing individual sign meanings from a living sign system — neither is fixable within the schedule.

### 6.3 The case against the runner-up, **B — "The Absent Object"**

B is the more interesting document and it would be the better *exhibition*. I would not build it, for five reasons:

1. **It centres the limitation rather than the work.** The brief's guiding principle is *"Do not merely display the past. Make people feel its influence on the present."* B's implicit thesis is "we don't have the pictures, and that is the story." That is a rigorous thing to make — and it makes the institution the subject.
2. **It is a single reading, and it is fragile.** A visitor who does not decode the voids sees an unfinished page. A has multiple ways to be understood; B has one.
3. **Its cost is copy, and copy is not budgeted.** Every void carries the entire page. SKU §8 assigns one designer/developer and no writer.
4. **It has the worst degradation path**, which is the wrong property for a 10-day build with a performance requirement.
5. **It sits closest to the SKU §6 exclusion** of "a portfolio or agency aesthetic." Conceptual exhibition design built as a demonstration of an idea is what agencies publish; B would have to fight that perception at every step.

The one thing B gets right — **never let an absence be silent** — is imported into A, where it costs almost nothing.

### 6.4 Why not **C**, and what survives from it

C has the best *historical* argument of the three (Nsibidi's documented continuity from the Cross River region to Cuba and Haiti; Uli's ordering principle) and the best structural answer to "Stories Through Time." It loses on three counts that are decisive here: it is not buildable to standard in the budget; it is the hardest of the three to make accessible; and it asks the project to use a **living, meaning-bearing sign system** as interface furniture — the very anti-pattern the research names, with the added offence of doing it with a real system rather than an invented one. Every mark would need its meaning individually sourced, and that dependency cannot be closed inside this stage.

Its two best contributions — **registered states as the identity mechanic**, and **a marker system for the disciplines** — are absorbed into A, where they are safe, cheap, and testable.

### 6.5 What the Project Manager should escalate

Three things need a decision or a check that I cannot make alone:

1. **Typeface licensing budget (zero to low).** The recommendation runs entirely on SIL Open Font License faces, which is the correct answer at this budget and is not a compromise. **But `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` R2 ("the typeface is an artefact… justified from Nigerian print history") cannot be satisfied as written**, because I could not verify the existence of a Nigerian-designed typeface suitable for this brief (§12). I have satisfied R2's *intent* two other ways (script competence + explicit on-page credit). If the PM believes R2 is binding, that is a research task, not a design task, and it needs its own stage.
2. **Confirmation that the Met's Idia image is used at most once**, in the Design department, with its 1897 loot history named. This is a cultural decision, not only a rights one.
3. **Confirmation of the honesty-marker system (A8)** as a page-wide requirement. It is the single most distinctive thing in this document, and it only works if it is applied to every claim rather than to a subset.

### 7.4 Motion

```
--dur-1: 120ms;   /* hover / focus feedback */
--dur-2: 240ms;   /* state change: marker rule, link underline */
--dur-3: 420ms;   /* entrance: the press/print wipe */
--dur-4: 720ms;   /* section transition */
--dur-5: 1200ms;  /* masthead state progression — the slow register */

--ease-standard: cubic-bezier(.2, .6, .2, 1);
--ease-out:      cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1);
--ease-in-out:   cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1);
--ease-press:    cubic-bezier(.3, 1.4, .4, 1);   /* printing reveal ONLY */

--motion-shift:  12px;   /* maximum entrance translate. Never more. */
--stagger:       60ms;   /* per item, maximum 6 items */
```

**Binding motion rules.**
- One `IntersectionObserver` (threshold `0.2`), one stagger utility. No scroll listeners for layout.
- Registered wordmark states. **Four states, named, finite. Never arbitrary.**
- Animate **`transform` and `opacity` only.** Never `width`, `height`, `top`, `left`, or `filter`.
- `will-change` on at most 3 nodes at once, removed after transition.
- `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` → all durations `1ms`, all translates `0`, masthead pinned to `state 01 SOLID`. **This is a first-class state, not a fallback.**
- Each entrance animation fires **once**; never re-triggered on scroll-back.
- Motion is never on the critical path: nothing essential is hidden until an animation runs.

## 9. Where this could go generic

The honest section. Each direction names the clichés it flirts with, and the specific rule that stops it.

### 9.1 Direction A flirts with four

| Cliché | How A avoids it |
| --- | --- |
| **"Dark editorial with a big sans-serif"** — the default contemporary-culture-website look, 2020–2026. | The **honesty markers** and the **plate sections** have no equivalent on any comparable site. If those are cut, A *is* the cliché, and should be cut with them. |
| **"Archival = a texture + a serif + a drop cap."** | Archival is treated as a *system* property — folios, ironed credits, accession numbers, confidence markers — not as a visual effect. `LOGO_RESEARCH.md` §13.3 says it exactly: *"Archival is a system property, not a colour."* No grain, no dust, no vignette, no faux paper texture. |
| **"African = warm earth tones + pattern."** | The palette is grounded in three *specific, documented* sources: press ink, the adire indigo vat, and Benin bronze. There is no pattern anywhere in the system. The one warm accent is a bronze, and it is cited. |
| **"Serif = museum template"** (SKU §6's own banned template: beige, serif, grid-of-cards). | The masthead and *all* display are grotesque; Charis is confined to running text and quotations; Newsprint appears once; there are no cards at all. The rule is machine-checkable in review: **if a serif is doing display work, or a card is filled with a discipline colour, the direction has been violated.** |

### 9.2 Direction B flirts with four

| Cliché | How B avoids it |
| --- | --- |
| **"Minimalism as an excuse for an empty page."** | A void is never empty — it is *measured*, *dimensioned*, *named*, *attributed* and *rights-statused*. The density is in the annotation, not the image. If the label is missing, it is not a void, it is a bug — and the spec says so. |
| **"Gallery aesthetic"** — spotlights, white cube, or black cube as a mood. | There is no spotlight, no gradient, no vignette and no cube. There is **one warm black, one brass accent, and a floor plan.** The environmental quality comes from scale and wayfinding, not from lighting effects. |
| **"Retro poster nostalgia"** — the Cooper-Series register that Fraunces can slide into. | Fraunces is pinned: `WONK 0`, `SOFT 0`, max `opsz`, weight 300–400. Approaching the friendly-retro axis is a spec violation, not a taste call. |
| **"Absence as a design-school conceit."** | Guarded by the direction's own worst-case test: **if a reader cannot tell a deliberate void from a broken image within two seconds, the void is not labelled well enough.** That is a build acceptance test, not an aspiration. |

### 9.3 Direction C flirts with four

| Cliché | How C avoids it |
| --- | --- |
| **"Generative = agency R&D."** SKU §6 excludes the portfolio/agency aesthetic outright. | The system is bounded by **four named states, three era registers, and four discipline marks**. Nothing is procedurally random. Randomness here would be the cliché; **ordering** is the idea (and it is the citable Uli principle). The rule: *no value in the system is generated at runtime that could not have been specified.* |
| **"African pattern wallpaper."** | There is no repeat, no tile, no texture and no woven grid. The one pattern in the system is a **halftone dot-screen of the brand's own ink** — a printing artefact, not a textile quotation. |
| **"Timeline gradient"** — the era strip that says nothing. | The three era registers are **discrete and hard-boundaried at named dates**. A continuous gradient between eras is explicitly banned by the direction's colour rules. |
| **"Motion for its own sake."** | Three concurrent animated properties maximum; one observer; one rAF loop; every moving thing maps to a stated meaning (state, era, ordering). Anything that moves without a meaning attached is removed. |

### 9.4 The cliché all three share, and the rule that covers them

All three flirt with **"specificity theatre"**: listing Nigerian nouns — *adire*, *aso oke*, *jùjú*, *highlife*, *FESTAC* — as texture rather than as content. It looks specific and conveys nothing.

**The single rule covering all three directions:** *every proper noun on the page must be attached to a date, an actor, and a source, or it comes off the page.* This is enforceable directly against `CONTENT_REGISTER.md`, it is required by SKU §8 anyway, and it is — more than any visual device here — the thing that makes ARCHIVE specific rather than generically cultural.

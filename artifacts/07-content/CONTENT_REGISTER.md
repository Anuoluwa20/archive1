# CONTENT REGISTER — ARCHIVE

**Purpose.** Verified factual backbone for the ARCHIVE landing page. Every claim below
was read on the page named in its Source URL. Nothing here is inferred, combined across
sources, or promoted from SKU §11 without a source.

**Hard rule applied:** a fact appears only if the fetched page states it. Where a source
is weak, self-contradictory, or carries a content warning, that is stated in the row.

**What went in.** Figures and moments with a clear date and a clear actor, pulled from
Wikipedia articles and, where possible, corroborated against institutional collection
APIs: **MoMA**, **The Met** (public collection API), **Art Institute of Chicago** (public
API) and the **V&A** (public API). Institutional corroboration is what lifts an entry to
*High* confidence.

**What I could not verify.** Large parts of SKU §11 survive only as names:
the "Lagos studio portrait tradition" as a *named* tradition, FESTAC '77 as a
*photography documentation* project, "Nigerian modernism in book and record sleeve
design" (book publishing yes — record sleeve design no), the "Nsukka School" as a
standalone entity, Akinola Lasekan, Don Barber and Amos Olarenwaju Osidele. Full list in
§5.

**Single biggest gap.** **Nigerian graphic design proper — posters, book jackets, record
sleeves, signage, type.** The design table is held up by the Zaria/Nsukka art-school
lineage, the Mbari Club's publishing programme, one emblem (FESTAC '77) and one building
(the National Theatre). That is art, publishing and architecture standing in for design.
No source I could reach documents Nigerian record sleeve design or poster practice as a
history, and SKU §11 asserts it exists. This is the one part of the four-discipline
structure the register cannot currently carry honestly.

**Coverage limitation — state this plainly.** Web search is **not available** in this
environment (no search provider configured). I fetched known URLs directly: Wikipedia via
the MediaWiki raw endpoint and API, plus the Met, AIC and V&A collection APIs. Consequences:

- **Smithsonian / National Museum of African Art** — blocked (HTTP 403).
- **British Museum** collection pages — blocked (HTTP 403); the Benin ivory mask is
  therefore cited via Wikipedia + the Met API.
- **Met website HTML** — behind a bot checkpoint; the Met's **API** worked normally.
- Coverage is therefore **Anglophone-internet shaped**. Nigerian institutional sources
  (National Museum Lagos, Centre for Contemporary Art Lagos, Terra Kulture, Nigerian
  archives) were only reachable where Wikipedia cites them; I could not open them directly.
  Newspaper citations below are read *as cited by Wikipedia*, and are marked accordingly.

## 6. Spotlight candidates

Eight figures/moments strong enough to carry the Featured Creator / Cultural Moment
section. Ordered by how much verified material exists.

1. **J.D. 'Okhai Ojeikere (1930–2014) — the hairstyles.** The single best-documented
   subject: a named project with a start date (1968), a body of work (≈1,000 hairstyle
   prints; 10,000+ photograph archive), and four institutional holdings verified by API
   (MoMA, the Met, Art Institute of Chicago, the V&A) plus the 2013 Venice Biennale
   Arsenale. Nothing needs asserting — it is all checkable.
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._D._%27Okhai_Ojeikere ·
   https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/44110 ·
   https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1/objects/542418

2. **Fela Kuti and the Africa '70 lineage (from 1968).** Dates, the Kalakuta Republic
   (1970), the Afrika Shrine residency (1970–75), the 1977 raid, the 1984 imprisonment —
   a fully dated political and musical story. Tony Allen gives it a second named actor and
   a quotable line ("without Tony Allen, there would be no Afrobeat"), and the
   coexistence of *Afrobeat* and *Afrobeats* gives the page a real past→present move.
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fela_Kuti ·
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Allen_(musician) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrobeat

3. **Shade Thomas-Fahm (born 1933).** The only fashion figure with **institutional
   holdings** backing her: 16 V&A objects including a named, accessioned piece
   ('Princess Kaftan', T.5-2020, given by the designer), plus the V&A's own framing as
   "Nigeria's first fashion designer" and a 2022–23 exhibition to hang it on.
   Sources: https://api.vam.ac.uk/v2/museumobject/O1616494 ·
   https://www.vam.ac.uk/exhibitions/africa-fashion

4. **FESTAC '77 (15 Jan – 12 Feb 1977).** Carries all four disciplines at once — music
   programme with 56 nations, a designed identity (Emokpae's ivory-mask emblem), and the
   National Theatre built for it. Best "cultural moment" candidate by breadth.
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FESTAC_77 ·
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhabor_Emokpae

5. **The Zaria Art Society and natural synthesis (1958–1962).** A dated movement with
   named actors who between them generated the Nsukka School, the Mbari Club and Venice's
   2023 Golden Lion. Gives the page an explicit "decolonising the curriculum" idea, which
   is contemporary without being invented.
   Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaria_Art_Society ·
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uche_Okeke · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demas_Nwoko

6. **King Sunny Adé (born 1946).** A clean international-breakthrough story: band from
   1967, Island Records in 1982, *Juju Music* (1982), *Synchro System* (1983), Grammy
   nomination. The "first Nigerian Grammy nominee" claim must be attributed, not stated
   as fact.
   Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Sunny_Ad%C3%A9

7. **Sikiru Ayinde Barrister and fuji (1948–2010).** Fuji has an etymological origin story
   with a specific date and actor (named after a Mount Fuji poster), a career from 1965
   and 70+ albums — and it is the least-covered of the four music traditions, so it adds

**B. Things that look like common myths or are contested — flag on the page:**

- **"Highlife is Nigerian."** The source says highlife is a **Ghanaian** genre that
  originated in 19th-century coastal Ghana. What is Nigerian is **Igbo highlife**, which
  became Nigeria's most popular genre in the 1960s. SKU §11 lists highlife under Nigerian
  music without this distinction — the page should make it.
- **"Afrobeat and Afrobeats are the same thing."** Both source pages say explicitly that
  they are not, and that they are often conflated. This is a live trap and also a good
  story beat.
- **"Fela invented Afrobeat alone."** The sources credit Fela as pioneer **with** Tony
  Allen, and quote Fela himself: "without Tony Allen, there would be no Afrobeat."
- **"Ankara is traditional African cloth."** It is industrially produced cotton introduced
  by **Dutch** merchants in the 19th century, from Indonesian **batik**. The name derives
  from the Hausa word for **Accra**.
- **"Adire means tie and dye."** The sourced etymology is *adi* ("to tie") + *re* ("to
  soak"). The "tie and dye" gloss is common but is not what the source says.
- **"Jùjú music is juju (the religion)."** The name came from a colonial term for African
  spiritual practices, applied because of beliefs around the tambourine; the music is not
  a religious practice. Any copy implying a link to juju as occult practice is wrong.
- **"Thomas-Fahm is the first Nigerian fashion designer" — as bare fact.** It is a
  *claim*, made by the V&A and repeated by Wikipedia ("regarded as the first"). Attribute
  it ("described by the V&A as…"), don't assert it.
- **"King Sunny Adé was the first Nigerian Grammy nominee."** Stated on the page, in two
  places, but I reached no Grammy/institutional source. Attribute or soften.
- **"Ojeikere's hair series began in the 1950s."** The same Wikipedia page contradicts
  itself (1950s vs 1968). Use 1968.
- **The name "Tunde King" appears in two different contexts.** The jùjú article makes
  Tunde King (Abdulrafiu Babatunde King) the 1920s originator of jùjú; the fuji article
  lists "Tunde King" among those popularising **wéré** in the 1950s. These may be two
  different people, or a conflation. **Do not merge them into one biography.**
- **Palm-wine music's date.** The article's infobox (Kru people, 1920s) contradicts its
  body (late 19th century, coastal Ghana/Sierra Leone/Liberia/Nigeria). Use the body text
  or avoid the precise date.
- **Nike Davies-Okundaye's "first solo exhibition, 1968."** Sits badly with a 1951 birth
  date. Needs a second source before it goes on the page.

**C. Structural caveat for whoever writes the copy.**

The four disciplines are not equally documented. Photography and Music have depth
(institutional holdings, dated movements, multiple named actors). Fashion has one
well-documented figure and good object-level data. **Design is thin and skewed towards
fine art and one building.** If the page presents all four as equally evidenced, it will
be overclaiming on Design. Either source Design properly, or let the section be honest
that it is a starting point.
